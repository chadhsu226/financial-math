Financial Mathematics Chapter 6: Return, Risk, and Quantitative Portfolio Analysis

Introduction

The first five chapters focused on cash flows and valuation under certainty. This chapter introduces the core feature of financial markets — uncertainty (that is, risk). We will learn how to use basic statistical tools to precisely quantify a single asset's expected return and risk, and understand how a portfolio can effectively diversify away risk — the foundation of modern investment theory.

6.1 Measuring and Expecting Returns

Expected Return Formula

$$\bar{R} = \sum_{i=1}^{n} P_i \times R_i$$

6.2 Quantifying Risk: Variance and Standard Deviation

Variance ($\sigma^2$) Formula

$$\sigma^2 = \sum_{i=1}^{n} P_i \times (R_i - \bar{R})^2$$

Standard Deviation ($\sigma$) Formula

$$\sigma = \sqrt{\sigma^2}$$

Worked Example 6.2: Calculating Risk (Asset A)

Continuing Example 6.1, with $\bar{R}_A = 8.5\%$:

$$\sigma^2_A = 0.009525$$
$$\sigma_A = \sqrt{0.009525} \approx 9.76\%$$

6.3 Portfolios and Diversification

Portfolio Expected Return

$$\bar{R}_P = \sum_{j=1}^{m} w_j \times \bar{R}_j$$

Portfolio Variance (Two-Asset Formula)

$$\sigma_P^2 = w_A^2 \sigma_A^2 + w_B^2 \sigma_B^2 + 2 w_A w_B \sigma_A \sigma_B \rho_{AB}$$

Correlation Coefficient ($\rho_{AB}$)

$$\rho_{AB} = \frac{\text{Cov}_{AB}}{\sigma_A \sigma_B}$$
  • $\rho_{AB} = +1.0$: The assets move perfectly together — no diversification benefit.
  • $\rho_{AB} = -1.0$: The assets move perfectly opposite each other — risk can be fully eliminated (at the right weights).
  • $\rho_{AB} = 0$: The assets are unrelated, giving a meaningful diversification benefit.

Illustrating the Diversification Effect

With each asset at $50\%$ of the portfolio and $\rho_{AB}=0$ (fully independent):

$$\sigma_P = \sqrt{0.0047625} \approx 6.90\% < \sigma_A = 9.76\%$$

While the expected return stays at $8.5\%$, risk falls from $9.76\%$ to $6.90\%$ — a clear demonstration of the power of diversification.
