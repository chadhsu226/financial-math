FINANCIAL MATHEMATICS / CHAPTER 1

1.1 Simple interest

Interest is calculated only on the original principal. With a fixed rate, annual interest stays constant.

I = P × r × n
FV = P + I = P(1 + rn)

For principal of 8,000 at 5% per year, annual interest is always 400.

FINANCIAL MATHEMATICS / CHAPTER 1

A simple interest calculation

Principal: 8,000. Annual rate: 5%. Term: 4 years.

I = 8,000 × 0.05 × 4 = 1,600
FV = 8,000 + 1,600 = 9,600

For a six-month deposit, use n = 0.5 rather than 6.

I = 8,000 × 0.05 × 0.5 = 200

FINANCIAL MATHEMATICS / CHAPTER 1

Compounding frequency and the periodic rate

FV = P(1 + r / m)mn
  • r is the nominal annual rate; m is compounding periods per year.
  • Periodic rate = r / m; total periods = mn.
  • At a 5% nominal annual rate, compounded monthly for 4 years: use 0.05/12 per period and 48 periods.

1.4 Nominal and effective annual rates

  • Nominal annual rate r: combine it with m to determine the periodic rate.
  • Effective annual rate (EAR): the one-year growth rate including compounding.
  • EAR differs from the real interest rate, which adjusts for inflation.
EAR = (1 + r / m)m − 1
FINANCIAL MATHEMATICS / CHAPTER 1

Example 1.3 Nominal 12%, compounded monthly

r / m = 0.12 / 12 = 0.01
EAR = 1.0112 − 1 = 12.6825%

Compounding at 1% per month produces approximately 12.68% growth over one year.

For continuous compounding: EAR = eʳ − 1.

FINANCIAL MATHEMATICS / CHAPTER 1

Converting an EAR to a monthly rate

When the quoted rate is an EAR, dividing by 12 does not give the equivalent monthly rate.

imonth = (1 + EAR)1/12 − 1
EAR = 12%　⇒　imonth ≈ 0.9489%

Distinguish the quotes: nominal 12% compounded monthly means 1% per month; EAR 12% means approximately 0.9489% per month.
