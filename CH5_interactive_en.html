Core Concepts at a Glance

Definitions, pros and cons, and decision criteria for capital budgeting methods.

Core idea: The present value today of a project's discounted future cash flows, measuring the absolute increase in shareholder wealth.

Formula: $NPV=\sum_{t=0}^{n}\tfrac{CF_{t}}{(1+r)^{t}}$

Criterion: Accept if $\text{NPV} > 0$.

Core idea: The discount rate that makes $\text{NPV}$ equal to zero, representing the annualized rate of return the project is expected to achieve.

Criterion: Accept if $\text{IRR} > \text{r}$ (cost of capital).

Note: When it conflicts with $\text{NPV}$ (e.g. for mutually exclusive projects), $\text{NPV}$ should take priority.

Core idea: Measures the time required to recover the initial investment; a liquidity indicator.

Drawback: Ignores the time value of money (the discounted payback period compensates for this) and ignores cash flows after the payback period.

Criterion: A shorter payback period is better, and it must be below a set threshold.
