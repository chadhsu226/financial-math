Chapter 4: Amortization and Loan Repayment

Introduction

This chapter applies the annuity present value concepts from Chapter 2 to real-world lending scenarios. You will learn how to calculate the fixed, equal payment amount for a loan, and how to build an amortization schedule to understand how each payment is split between interest and principal.

4.1 Determining the Loan Payment ($C$)

Core Concept

The loan principal ($PV$) is, in essence, the present value today of a future series of equal payments (an ordinary annuity, $C$). We can therefore rearrange the ordinary annuity present value formula to solve for the payment amount ($C$) per period.

1. Loan Payment Formula

$$C = PV_A \times \frac{r}{1 - (1+r)^{-n}}$$

2. Worked Example 4.1: Calculating a Mortgage Payment

Scenario: A loan of $3{,}000{,}000$, at an annual rate of $2.4\%$, compounded monthly, over a $20$-year term ($240$ months). What is the monthly payment?

Periodic rate $r = 2.4\%/12 = 0.002$, total periods $n = 240$

$$C = 3{,}000{,}000 \times \frac{0.002}{1 - (1.002)^{-240}} \approx 15{,}751.34$$

4.2 The Amortization Schedule

Core Concept

An amortization schedule details how each payment splits into interest and principal. With a fixed payment, as the balance shrinks, the interest portion decreases each period while the principal portion increases.

Steps to Build the Schedule

  • Interest for the period: $Interest_t = \text{beginning balance} \times r$
  • Principal repaid: $Principal_t = C - Interest_t$
  • Ending balance: $\text{ending balance}_t = \text{beginning balance}_t - Principal_t$

4.3 Calculating the Outstanding Loan Balance

After making payments for a while, borrowers often want to know "how much do I still owe?" — for example, before paying off early, refinancing or selling a home. The principal still unpaid after the $k$th payment is the outstanding loan balance (OLB). There are two ways to compute it: the forward-looking prospective method and the backward-looking retrospective method.

1. The Prospective Method: PV of the Remaining Payments

After period $k$, the borrower still has $(n-k)$ payments of $C$ to make. The amount the bank would accept to settle the loan at that point is the present value of those remaining payments at period $k$:

$$\text{OLB}_k = C \times \left[ \frac{1 - (1+r)^{-(n-k)}}{r} \right]$$

2. Worked Example 4.3: Remaining Balance by the Prospective Method

Continuing Example 4.1, what is the remaining loan balance after $5$ years of payments ($k=60$ periods)? There are $240-60=180$ payments left:

$$\text{OLB}_{60} = 15{,}751.34 \times \left[ \frac{1 - (1.002)^{-180}}{0.002} \right] \approx 2{,}379{,}025.69$$

Even after $5$ years of payments (a total of $60 \times 15{,}751.34 = 945{,}080.40$), only about $620{,}974.31$ of principal has been repaid; the rest was interest — a clear illustration that most of the early payments on a loan go toward interest.

3. The Retrospective Method: FV of the Loan minus FV of Payments Made

We can also look back. Had nothing been repaid, the principal $PV$ would grow to $PV(1+r)^k$ by period $k$. But the borrower has already made $k$ payments of $C$, whose value at period $k$ is the annuity future value $C \times FVA$. The difference is what is still owed at period $k$:

$$\text{OLB}_k = PV(1+r)^k - C \times \left[ \frac{(1+r)^k - 1}{r} \right]$$

This is exactly what the amortization schedule in Section 4.2 does period by period ("opening balance + interest − payment"), so the retrospective balance matches the schedule's closing balance at period $k$.

Why do the two methods agree? When the loan is made, the principal equals the present value of all payments, which can be split into "the $k$ payments already made" and "the $n-k$ payments still to come":

$$PV = \sum_{t=1}^{k} \frac{C}{(1+r)^t} + \sum_{t=k+1}^{n} \frac{C}{(1+r)^t}$$

Multiply both sides by $(1+r)^k$ (moving everything to period $k$) and move the payments already made to the left: the left side is the retrospective method, and the right side is the prospective method.

4. Worked Example 4.4: Checking Example 4.3 with the Retrospective Method

Same loan: $3{,}000{,}000$, monthly rate $0.002$, monthly payment $15{,}751.34$. Find the balance after $k=60$ periods:

  1. Principal grown to period 60: $3{,}000{,}000 \times (1.002)^{60} \approx 3{,}382{,}085.22$
  2. FV of the 60 payments made: $15{,}751.34 \times \left[\dfrac{(1.002)^{60} - 1}{0.002}\right] \approx 1{,}003{,}059.04$
  3. Subtract: $\text{OLB}_{60} \approx 3{,}382{,}085.22 - 1{,}003{,}059.04 = 2{,}379{,}026.18$
A common mistake: subtracting the present value of the payments made from the principal: $3{,}000{,}000 - 15{,}751.34 \times \left[\dfrac{1-(1.002)^{-60}}{0.002}\right] \approx 2{,}110{,}259.82$. That figure is a value today (period 0), not the balance at period 60. Both amounts must be at the same point in time before subtracting, so multiply by $(1.002)^{60}$: $2{,}110{,}259.82 \times (1.002)^{60} \approx 2{,}379{,}026.18$.

Why does this differ from Example 4.3 by $0.49$? Because the monthly payment was rounded to $15{,}751.34$. With the unrounded payment, both methods give $2{,}379{,}026.04$. In practice, the bank adjusts the final payment slightly to absorb the difference.
