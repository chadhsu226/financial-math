Chapter 2: Cash Flows and Annuities

Introduction

Having mastered the time value of a single cash flow, this chapter moves closer to real-world financial situations: handling a series of equal, recurring cash flows, known as an annuity. You will learn to apply present value and future value principles to compute the total value of such cash flow series, laying the groundwork for more complex financial planning.

2.1 Cash Flow Basics

Core Concept

A cash flow is money that actually flows in or out at a specific point in time. Accurately identifying both the amount and the timing of each cash flow is essential.

  • Inflow (+): Money coming in, shown as a positive value.
  • Outflow (−): Money going out, shown as a negative value.

2. Worked Example 2.1: Using a Timeline

Scenario: Today you buy a new laptop for $5{,}000$, and for the next three years you will spend $1{,}000$ on software at the start of each year.

Timeline: $t=0$ is $-\$5{,}000$ (outflow); $t=1, 2, 3$ are each $-\$1{,}000$.

2.2 Defining and Classifying Annuities

Core Concept

An annuity is a series of equal cash flows occurring at regular intervals.

Ordinary Annuity: Cash flows occur at the end of each period. Example: the monthly payments on a mortgage or car loan.

Annuity Due: Cash flows occur at the beginning of each period. Example: rent payments, or a savings plan that begins at the start of each month.

Every cash flow of an annuity due arrives one period earlier than in an ordinary annuity, earning one extra period of interest, so its value is $(1+r)$ times that of the ordinary annuity.

Perpetuity: Equal cash flows that continue forever. Example: the fixed dividend paid by certain preferred shares.

2.3 Future Value of an Annuity

1. Future Value of an Ordinary Annuity ($FVA$)

$$FVA = C\left[\frac{(1+r)^n - 1}{r}\right]$$

2. Future Value of an Annuity Due ($FVAD$)

$$FVAD = C\left[\frac{(1+r)^n - 1}{r}\right](1+r)$$

3. Worked Example 2.2: Future Value of an Annuity Due

Scenario: Every year, at the start of the year, Alex deposits $C=10{,}000$ at an annual rate of $r=4\%$, for $n=8$ years. What is the future value of these savings after $8$ years?

$$FVAD = 10000\left[\frac{(1.04)^8 - 1}{0.04}\right](1.04) \approx 95{,}827.95$$

2.4 Present Value of an Annuity

1. Present Value of an Ordinary Annuity ($PVA$)

$$PVA = C\left[\frac{1 - \frac{1}{(1+r)^n}}{r}\right]$$

2. Present Value of an Annuity Due ($PVAD$)

$$PVAD = PVA \times (1+r)$$

3. Present Value of a Perpetuity ($PVP$)

$$PVP = \frac{C}{r}$$

4. Worked Example 2.3: Present Value of a Mortgage (Ordinary Annuity)

Scenario: Maya can afford to pay $C=25{,}000$ per month toward a mortgage. The annual rate is $1.8\%$, compounded monthly, over a $20$-year term. What is the total loan amount she can qualify for?

(Monthly rate $r_m = 0.0015$, total periods $N = 240$)

$$PVA = 25{,}000\left[\frac{1 - \frac{1}{(1+0.0015)^{240}}}{0.0015}\right] \approx 5{,}035{,}591.07$$
