Capital budgeting is the process by which firms plan and manage long-term investment projects; NPV and IRR are the two most central evaluation metrics.

FINANCIAL MATHEMATICS / CHAPTER 5

5.1 Net Present Value (NPV)

Net present value is the gold standard for evaluating project feasibility: it measures the net value today of all of a project's future cash inflows and outflows after discounting.

NPV = Σt=0n CFt / (1 + r)t
FINANCIAL MATHEMATICS / CHAPTER 5

NPV decision rule

  • If NPV > 0: the project is acceptable and will increase shareholder wealth.
  • If NPV < 0: the project is unacceptable and should be rejected.
  • If NPV = 0: the project only breaks even.
FINANCIAL MATHEMATICS / CHAPTER 5

Example 5.1: Calculating NPV

Initial investment of 100,000. Net cash inflow of 40,000 per year for the next three years. Cost of capital r = 10%.

NPV = −100,000 + 40,000/1.10 + 40,000/1.102 + 40,000/1.103
NPV ≈ −525.92

Decision: since NPV < 0, the project is unacceptable and should be rejected.

FINANCIAL MATHEMATICS / CHAPTER 5

5.2 Internal Rate of Return (IRR)

IRR is the discount rate at which a project's NPV is exactly zero; it represents the annualized return the project is expected to achieve.

Σt=0n CFt / (1 + IRR)t = 0
  • If IRR > r (cost of capital): the project is acceptable.
  • If IRR < r (cost of capital): the project is unacceptable.
FINANCIAL MATHEMATICS / CHAPTER 5

Example 5.2: Estimating and Applying IRR

Continuing Example 5.1 (initial investment of 100,000, with inflows of 40,000 per year for the next three years), solve by trial and error or a numerical method:

IRR ≈ 9.70%

Decision: IRR = 9.70% < r = 10%, so the project is unacceptable — consistent with the NPV conclusion.

5.3 Supplementary Capital Budgeting Methods

Payback Period Method

  • Measures the time required to recover the initial investment cost.
  • Drawback: ignores the time value of money.

Discounted Payback Period Method

  • Accounts for the time value of money, measuring the time needed to recover the discounted value of the investment.
FINANCIAL MATHEMATICS / CHAPTER 5

Example 5.3: Calculating the Payback Period

Initial investment of 250,000, cost of capital r = 12%, with cash inflows over the next four years of 100,000, 120,000, 80,000, and 50,000.

Payback Period = 2 + 30,000 / 80,000 = 2.375 years
Discounted Payback Period ≈ 3.255 years

Cumulative inflows over the first two years total 220,000, leaving 30,000 short of full recovery; the third year's inflow is 80,000, so the payback point falls at 0.375 into the third year.

FINANCIAL MATHEMATICS / CHAPTER 5

Comprehensive Exercise 3

Explain the core difference between NPV and IRR, and why the NPV rule should take priority when the two conflict.

Show solution

NPV measures the absolute increase in wealth a project creates (a dollar amount), while IRR measures a relative rate of return (a percentage). When comparing mutually exclusive projects that differ greatly in scale, a smaller project with a higher IRR may create far less absolute wealth than a larger project with a lower IRR. Since the ultimate goal of corporate decision-making is to maximize shareholder wealth in dollar terms rather than to maximize the percentage rate of return, NPV should govern whenever the two conflict.

Show solution

Chapter summary

  • NPV is the gold standard for evaluating project feasibility: a project is acceptable only if NPV > 0, and the value represents the absolute increase in shareholder wealth.
  • IRR is the discount rate that sets NPV to zero; it must be compared with the cost of capital, and a project is acceptable only if IRR > r.
  • The payback period and discounted payback period are supplementary indicators that quickly gauge how fast capital is recovered, but they cannot replace NPV as the primary decision criterion.
  • When the NPV and IRR conclusions for mutually exclusive projects conflict, NPV always governs, because it directly measures the increase in shareholder wealth.
