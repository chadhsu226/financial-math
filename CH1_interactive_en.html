Core Concepts at a Glance

A quick review of the most important definitions and formulas for the time value of money.

Core Idea: Interest is calculated only on the original principal ($P$); growth is linear.

Formulas:

  • $I = P \times r \times n$
  • $FV = P(1 + r \times n)$

Core Idea: "Interest on interest." Each period's interest is added back to the principal, and the next period's interest is calculated on the new principal, producing exponential growth.

Formulas:

  • General compounding: $FV = P(1+\frac{r}{m})^{mn}$
  • Continuous compounding: $FV = Pe^{rn}$

Core Idea: The reverse application of TVM. Converting a future value ($FV$) back into a present value ($PV$) is called discounting.

Formula: $PV=\frac{FV}{(1+r)^{n}}$

$r$ is the discount rate, typically the opportunity cost of capital or the required rate of return.

Core Idea: The actual annual rate earned or paid after accounting for the compounding frequency ($m$), used to compare the true cost or return of different products.

Formula: $EAR=(1+\frac{r_{\text{Nominal}}}{m})^{m}-1$

The nominal rate is the annual rate stated in the contract, without regard to compounding frequency.
