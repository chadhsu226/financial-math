FINANCIAL MATHEMATICS / CHAPTER 5

Introduction: what is capital budgeting?

Planning and evaluating long-term investment projects: new machines, factories, branches, product lines.

  • Large sums, long-lasting effects, hard to reverse.
  • The money goes out today; the payoffs arrive over many years, so they must be converted to present value before comparing.
  • Roadmap: cash flows and cost of capital → NPV → IRR → payback → NPV vs. IRR conflicts.

The cost of capital r: an opportunity cost

The cost of capital is the minimum return the company requires, and the discount rate r.

  • It is an opportunity cost: what the money could earn in other investments of similar risk.
  • A project that cannot earn its cost of capital is not worth doing.

5.2 Net Present Value (NPV)

Net present value is the gold standard for evaluating project feasibility: it measures the net value today of all of a project's future cash inflows and outflows after discounting.

NPV = Σt=0n CFt / (1 + r)t
FINANCIAL MATHEMATICS / CHAPTER 5

NPV decision rule

  • If NPV > 0: the project is acceptable and will increase shareholder wealth.
  • If NPV < 0: the project is unacceptable and should be rejected.
  • If NPV = 0: the project only breaks even.

Example 5.1: interpretation

  • The 120,000 of inflows is worth only 99,474.08 today — less than the 100,000 price.
  • NPV < 0: the oven earns slightly less than the required 10%, so do not buy.
  • Sensitivity: at 41,000 a year, NPV ≈ +1,960.93 and the decision flips to buy.

NPV is sensitive to cash flow forecasts; estimate them carefully.

FINANCIAL MATHEMATICS / CHAPTER 5

5.3 Internal Rate of Return (IRR)

IRR is the discount rate at which a project's NPV is exactly zero; it represents the annualized return the project is expected to achieve.

Σt=0n CFt / (1 + IRR)t = 0
  • If IRR > r (cost of capital): the project is acceptable.
  • If IRR < r (cost of capital): the project is unacceptable.
FINANCIAL MATHEMATICS / CHAPTER 5

The NPV profile: where is the IRR?

A higher discount rate means a lower NPV; the curve crosses NPV = 0 at the IRR. At r = 0%, NPV = 120,000 − 100,000 = 20,000.

FINANCIAL MATHEMATICS / CHAPTER 5

Example 5.2: trial and error and interpolation

  • r = 9%: NPV = 40,000 × [1 − (1.09)−3]/0.09 − 100,000 ≈ +1,251.79
  • r = 10%: NPV ≈ −525.92 → the IRR lies between 9% and 10%
IRR ≈ 9% + 1,251.79 / (1,251.79 + 525.92) × 1% ≈ 9.70%

Exact value ≈ 9.701%. Decision: IRR ≈ 9.70% < r = 10%, do not buy — the same as NPV.

FINANCIAL MATHEMATICS / CHAPTER 5

How NPV and IRR relate

  • For a conventional project (invest first, receive later), the NPV profile slopes down: IRR > r and NPV > 0 are the same statement.
  • For a single project the two rules agree.
  • For mutually exclusive projects they can conflict, and cash flows that change sign more than once can give several IRRs (see 5.5).

5.4 Supplementary methods: payback period

Payback Period Method

  • Measures the time required to recover the initial investment cost.
  • Drawback: ignores the time value of money.

Discounted Payback Period Method

  • Accounts for the time value of money, measuring the time needed to recover the discounted value of the investment.
FINANCIAL MATHEMATICS / CHAPTER 5

Example 5.3: Calculating the Payback Period

Initial investment of 250,000, cost of capital r = 12%, with cash inflows over the next four years of 100,000, 120,000, 80,000, and 50,000.

Payback Period = 2 + 30,000 / 80,000 = 2.375 years
Discounted Payback Period ≈ 3.255 years

Cumulative inflows over the first two years total 220,000, leaving 30,000 short of full recovery; the third year's inflow is 80,000, so the payback point falls at 0.375 into the third year.

5.5 Conflicts between NPV and IRR

For a single project the two rules agree; when choosing among mutually exclusive projects, they can disagree.

  • Common causes: differences in scale or in the timing of cash flows.
  • Rule: follow NPV, because it directly measures the increase in shareholder wealth.

FINANCIAL MATHEMATICS / CHAPTER 5

Comprehensive Exercise 6

Explain the core difference between NPV and IRR, and why the NPV rule should take priority when the two conflict.

Show solution

NPV measures the absolute increase in wealth a project creates (an amount), while IRR measures a relative rate of return (a percentage). Among mutually exclusive projects of very different scale, a small project with a higher IRR may create far less wealth than a larger project with a lower IRR. Since the goal is to maximize shareholder wealth, NPV governs when the two conflict.

FINANCIAL MATHEMATICS / CHAPTER 5

Chapter summary

  • Start with the cash flow timeline: initial investment negative, operating inflows positive; the cost of capital r is an opportunity cost and the discount rate.
  • NPV is the gold standard: discount period by period and add up; accept only if NPV > 0, which measures the gain in shareholder wealth.
  • The IRR is where the NPV profile crosses zero; estimate it by trial and error plus interpolation, and accept only if IRR > r.
  • Payback measures are supplementary; when NPV and IRR conflict for mutually exclusive projects, NPV governs.
