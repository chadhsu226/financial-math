Determining the Loan Payment (C)

The loan principal ($PV$) equals the present value of a series of future equal payments ($C$). This tool solves backward for the equal periodic payment.

Loan Parameter Input

$$\text{C} = PV_{A} \times \frac{r}{1 - (1+r)^{-N}}$$

Where $r$ is the periodic interest rate ($\frac{\text{annual rate}}{m}$), and $N$ is the total number of periods ($n \times m$)

Core Concepts at a Glance: Loan Amortization

A quick review of the key principles of loan repayment and amortization schedules.

Core concept: The loan ($PV_A$) is treated as the present value of a series of future equal payments ($C$), using a rearranged ordinary annuity present-value formula to solve for $C$.

Key point: Make sure the interest rate ($r$) and number of periods ($N$) match the number of payments per year ($m$) (e.g., monthly rate with total number of months).

Under an equal-payment structure, as time passes:

  • Interest this period $\downarrow$: Because the beginning balance ($OLB$) gradually decreases, the base on which interest is calculated shrinks.
  • Principal repaid $\uparrow$: Since $C$ is fixed, the portion no longer needed for interest automatically flows into principal repayment.
  • Early in the loan: Most of the payment goes toward interest.
